You join a call, and a moment later a new participant appears: *"Otter Notetaker has joined."* Convenient — someone's writing everything down. But that one line carries a set of trade-offs most people never stop to think about, and they're worth thirty seconds of your attention before you install one of these on every meeting you attend.

## What the bot actually changes

An AI note-taker that joins as a participant is doing two visible things: it's recording, and it's telling the room it's recording. That second part is a feature, not a bug. Consent is the whole ballgame with meeting recordings, and a visible bot puts consent on the record — everyone can see it's there.

The problem is the situations where you *don't* want a fourth "person" in the room. A candidate interview. A first call with a client. A difficult conversation with a report. In those moments, a bot silently listing itself as a participant can shift the tone of the meeting, and sometimes that's a price not worth paying for tidy notes.

## The bot-free alternative

This is the gap tools like Granola fill. Instead of joining the call, they listen to the audio coming through your own device and build notes from that — often shaped around what you typed during the meeting, rather than a generic wall-to-wall transcript. Nothing appears in the participant list. The meeting looks, to everyone else, like a normal meeting.

That's genuinely better for sensitive calls. But be clear about what it does and doesn't change: **you are still recording other people.** The same courtesy — and in many places, the same legal requirement — to tell people they're being recorded still applies. Bot-free changes who can *see* the recorder. It doesn't make recording consent-free.

## Where the audio goes

The less obvious question is what happens after the meeting. Most of these tools send your audio to their servers to transcribe and summarise it. For a weekly team standup, fine. For anything covered by an NDA, client confidentiality or a regulation like HIPAA or GDPR, that upload matters, and you should read the vendor's data-handling page rather than assume. A few tools are built specifically for this and carry the certifications to prove it; most aren't.

## So which should you use?

A simple rule covers most people: **visible bot for internal calls, bot-free for anything sensitive or external, and a compliance-certified tool if you're legally on the hook for the data.** If you want the specific tools that fit each of those — including the one we'd start with for everyday use — that's what the full comparison is for.
