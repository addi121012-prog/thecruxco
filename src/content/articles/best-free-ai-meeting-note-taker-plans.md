Almost every AI note-taker has a free plan, and almost every free plan is designed to run out at the exact moment the tool becomes a habit. That's not a reason to avoid them — starting free is genuinely the smart move — but it helps to know *which* wall you'll hit first, so you're not surprised into a subscription you didn't plan.

There are only three walls that matter.

## Wall 1: Recording minutes

The most common cap. Some tools give you a fixed number of minutes a month and stop there. Otter's free plan, for example, allows around 300 minutes a month — enough for a light week of calls, not enough if note-taking becomes your default. Fireflies frames its free limit around stored minutes rather than a monthly reset, which behaves differently: you're limited by your archive, not your calendar.

The standout here is Fathom, whose free plan records without a per-meeting cap. If your first worry is "will it just stop recording mid-month," that's the one to start with.

## Wall 2: AI features

The second wall is subtler. A tool might let you record freely but ration the actually-useful part — the AI-generated summaries, action items and ask-questions-about-the-call features. Fathom's free tier is generous on recording but limits how many AI summaries you get; that's the trade for no minute cap. So "unlimited free" almost always has an asterisk, and the asterisk is usually here.

## Wall 3: History

The one people notice too late. Some free plans quietly limit how far back your history goes, so recordings from a couple of months ago become inaccessible. If you treat your notes as a searchable archive you'll come back to, check the history limit *before* you rely on it — losing old transcripts is a bad surprise.

## How to choose a free plan

Match the plan to the wall you care least about hitting:

- **Worried about running out mid-month?** Start with the tool that doesn't cap recording minutes.
- **Only need notes occasionally?** A 300-minute plan is plenty, and you may never pay at all.
- **Building a long-term archive?** History limits matter more than minutes — check those first.

For most people the honest advice is simple: pick the most generous free plan, use it until you hit a specific wall, and only then decide whether that particular wall is worth paying to remove.
